So far this year, I'm amazed about how far we've come. Things that used to seem like they were outside of our reach are now firmly within our wheelhouse. Things that used to seem far off are now staring us squarely in the face. Software problems that used to be too fiddly to mess with and too complex to hand off to someone else just stuck around; now, we can start cracking on them! 

But that's not all; with these new-found abilities come many unrealistic expectations from business. And, these expectations do not usually take into account the actual capabilities of agentic AI. These unrealistic expectations come from a misunderstanding of a software engineer's role. There are so many dimensions to a software engineer's job. To boil down engineering to lines of code is like boiling down a writer's work to lines of text. Programmers are not "coders" like writers are not "typists". It's reductive.

And, that's just the start in a mismatch of values.

1. Do we prioritize delivering new features to the degradation of rigor?
2. Do we ignore agentic code reviews in a hand-wavy-looks-good-to-me kind of way?
3. Do we avoid wasting our time learning from the agents' work?
4. Do we care if our systems are secure, or are we just going to let the agents make the decisions for us?

Yikes! I'm sure you get the point. Agents super charge development but they do not remove the need for engineering oversight. I am convinced that we must stay **grounded** in our software engineering work if we are to build complex systems. 

Beyond all of these concerns over a mismatch in values came a more interesting discovery. It's obvious in retrospect, but it wasn't clear to me in the outset of the year:

> Engineers must have advanced language skills to prompt agents to transform complex solutions.

This echos the [Ironies of Automation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ironies_of_Automation) in that our skills as engineers are more important than they ever were before. We have to be able to identify the current state of the system, imagine the future of that system, and provide a precise prompt for the agent to perform the transform.

If we don't stay grounded in our work, then we will lose our language. **Our language will atrophy!**

I've already heard developers trip over their words about how the system they manage works. Let's never be caught in the trap of not understanding the code we deliver.
