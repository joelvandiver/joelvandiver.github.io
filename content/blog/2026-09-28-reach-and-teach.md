As I pointed out in an earlier post, [Stay Grounded](@/blog/2026-09-26-stay-grounded.md), our skills as software engineers are in even greater demand. How can we improve our skills in the relentless barrage of content produced by agents? Do we have any hope of truly learning a skill by reviewing code generated by LLMs?


In my years as a professional Math teacher, I know how important it is to pull out pencil and paper and work out the problem by hand. I fully embraced technology in teaching and exploring concepts, but I saw the merit (and the joy) of struggling through first hand.


In my 14 years of software engineering since, I can say the same is true for programming. At times, it is imperative to remove all of the noise of technology and struggle through the concepts at hand. The true power in programming are the ideas behind them, not the characters on the screen. 


I’ve come up with a simple system reminiscent of my days as a teacher. First, I have to struggle with the concepts from first principles. Then, I may work towards higher levels of abstraction and allow greater use of technology. 


I’ve built a system I call “REACH and TEACH” where I devote 10 mins blocks for each on various topics into my daily routine. I find that 10 mins is enough to get moving on a topic and make progress, but it also doesn’t take up too much of my day. I organize the start of my day around the topics I’m reaching for and I close out my day with the topics I’m solidifying through teaching.


For example, my task list might look like:

- REACH - Explore Rust STD IO Internals [*10 mins*]
- REACH - Build a Math Library in Rust from Scratch [*10 mins*]
- REACH - Build a microVM Platform in Rust [*10 mins*]
- TEACH - Build a Rust Intro to Programming Guide [*10 mins*]
- TEACH - Build a Postgres Intro to SQL Guide [*10 mins*]
- TEACH - Build a Kubernetes and Container Orchestration Guide [*10 mins*]


The REACH blocks are just slightly outside of my current abilities, while I use the TEACH blocks to solidify my understanding.


So, I turn on a timer for each and get kraken’!
